# Problem 03

## The original problem

For

$$\frac{x^2}{25}+\frac{y^2}{9}=1,$$

find the semiaxis lengths, the intercepts with the coordinate axes, and make a sketch. Explain along which axis the ellipse is more stretched and how this is visible in the equation.

## What we are looking for

We need the two semi-axis lengths, the four axis intercepts, and an explanation of the orientation.

## The idea we need

An ellipse centered at the origin has canonical form $\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1$. The semi-axis lengths are $a$ and $b$. The larger denominator gives the more stretched direction.

## Step-by-step solution

Here $a^2=25$, so $a=5$, and $b^2=9$, so $b=3$.

The $x$-intercepts occur when $y=0$: $\frac{x^2}{25}=1$, so $x=\pm5$. Points: $(5,0)$ and $(-5,0)$.

The $y$-intercepts occur when $x=0$: $\frac{y^2}{9}=1$, so $y=\pm3$. Points: $(0,3)$ and $(0,-3)$.

Since $5>3$, the ellipse is more stretched along the $x$-axis. This is visible because the denominator under $x^2$ is larger.

## Why each step is valid

Setting one variable to zero isolates the other, giving the intercepts directly from the canonical equation. The semi-axis length is the square root of the corresponding denominator.

## Final answer (in words)

The ellipse has semi-axes $5$ (horizontal) and $3$ (vertical), with intercepts $(\pm5,0)$ and $(0,\pm3)$. It is stretched more along the $x$-axis.

## Verification

Substitute $(5,0)$: $25/25+0=1$. Substitute $(0,3)$: $0+9/9=1$. Both lie on the curve, and the semi-axes are positive.

## Common mistakes

- Calling $25$ and $9$ the semi-axes instead of their square roots.
- Confusing which axis is more stretched.
- Forgetting the negative intercepts.

## How I would explain it in class

I read $a$ and $b$ as square roots of the denominators. The larger denominator means a longer semi-axis in that direction. The intercepts are found by setting the other variable to zero.
